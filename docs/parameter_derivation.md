## 2. The model and what it predicts

An ideal brushed DC motor in SI units: V = R i + Ke w (L neglected), tau = Kt i, and Ke = Kt = K numerically (V.s/rad = N.m/A). With a viscous loss Bm, the steady no-load point satisfies

- no-load speed: w_nl = V Kt / (R Bm + Kt Ke)
- no-load current: i_nl = Bm w_nl / Kt
- stall current: I_s = V / R
- output stall torque: tau_s,out = Kt I_s N eta

## 3. Why the existing sets cannot match everything

**Method A** (R = 1.6 ohm from 12 V / 7.5 A, Kt = 1.974 mN.m/A from reflecting the output stall torque with eta = 1, Ke = Kt): the stall figures come out right but the no-load speed is far off. With its own friction, the model predicts about **41,153 RPM** at **2.18 A** no-load current, against 18,000 RPM and 0.8 A on the datasheet. (Without friction the open-loop simulation gives 39,827 RPM, +121 %.)

**Method C** (A's R and Kt, Ke = 6.366 mN.V.s/rad from the no-load speed): the no-load speed is better, about **15,968 RPM** at 0.85 A (-11 %), but Ke/Kt = 3.22 breaks the SI identity Ke = Kt, and the reflected stall torque is **21.74 kg.cm** instead of 27.18 (-20 %) at eta = 0.8.

**Why no (R, Kt, Ke, eta) fits both points at once.** Require the no-load point and the stall point together with Ke = Kt = K, R = V / I_s, and the no-load balance V = R I0 + K w_0:

    R = V / I_s = 12 / 7.5 = 1.6 ohm
    K = (V - R I0) / w_0 = (12 - 1.6 x 0.8) / 1884.96 = 5.687 mV.s/rad
    Bm = K I0 / w_0 = 2.414 uN.m.s/rad

This motor has a stall torque at the motor shaft of K I_s = 0.04265 N.m, so at the output **N K I_s = 7.678 N.m = 78.29 kg.cm before gearbox losses**. The datasheet's 27.18 kg.cm is only reached with **eta = 2.6654 / 7.678 = 0.347**. A 180:1 gearbox with an efficiency of 35 % is possible but low, and far from the 0.70-0.85 typical range. So the datasheet figures {18,000 RPM at 0.8 A, 7.5 A stall, 27.18 kg.cm stall torque at the output, eta in 0.70-0.85} are **mutually inconsistent for an ideal motor**: at least one of them is not what it appears to be. The three alternatives below each relax a different one.

## 4. The alternative sets (all inactive; select with `--params`)

All share R = 1.6 ohm, Ke = Kt = K = 5.687 mV.s/rad (so the SI identity holds), Bm = 2.414 uN.m.s/rad (the whole 0.8 A no-load current is attributed to viscous friction; part of it is probably Coulomb/brush friction, so this is an upper bound for Bm). Jm stays at the 5e-7 kg.m^2 estimate. They differ in the gearbox efficiency:

| Set | What is relaxed | eta | Output stall torque predicted | Rated torque at I_s/2 predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `method_d` | the efficiency is fitted so the output stall torque is 27.18 kg.cm | **0.347** | 27.18 kg.cm | 13.59 kg.cm |
| `method_d2` | 27.18 kg.cm is read as a **practical (current-limited) rating**, not the theoretical stall torque; eta keeps its 0.80 estimate | 0.80 | 62.63 kg.cm theoretical (27.18 reached at **3.25 A**) | 31.32 kg.cm |
| `method_d3` | the **12.5 kg.cm rated torque** is the anchor, taken at half the stall current (an ASSUMED rated point) | **0.319** | 25.00 kg.cm | 12.50 kg.cm |

Note that `method_d` and `method_d3` land on nearly the same efficiency (0.347 and 0.319) from two independent datasheet torque figures, which is mild evidence that, if 7.5 A is truly the locked-rotor current, the gearbox is lossy (or the torque figures are practical ratings). Also, 151 g.cm at the motor shaft corresponds to a current of 2.60 A with K = 5.687 mV.s/rad, a plausible rated current, which supports reading the torque figures as rated rather than stall values (`method_d2`).

## 5. Do the alternatives change the closed-loop behaviour?

Fixed-PID sequence, 90 deg step, bare shaft (full tables in `results/parameter_sets/closed_loop_by_set.md`). Tuned gains (15 / 0 / 0.3):

| set | rise [s] | overshoot [%] | settle [s] | IAE [deg s] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| method_a | 0.117 | 23.70 | 0.602 | 13.79 |
| method_c (active) | 0.190 | 1.73 | 0.292 | 13.00 |
| method_d / d2 / d3 | 0.192 | 0.00 | 0.348 | 10.51 |

**The choice matters a lot, but mostly between Ke = Kt sets and not between the d-variants.** Method A (K too small, so little back-EMF damping) overshoots by about 24 points; the consistent, larger K of method D damps the response so there is no overshoot at all; method C sits between. On the bare shaft the three d-variants are **identical** because the gearbox efficiency only acts on load torque. With a gravity-loaded arm (1 kg x 10 cm, Tuned gains, P+D only so a steady-state error remains) they separate: SSE -16.2 deg (d), -7.25 deg (d2), -17.5 deg (d3), against -19.8 deg for method C, and the torque margin ranges from 2.17 (A, C) to 6.26 (d2). So: closed-loop behaviour of an unloaded joint is **strongly dependent on K** (the Ke/Kt question), and loaded behaviour additionally depends on **eta**. Neither discrepancy can be dismissed as "small".

## 6. What to measure to settle it (one session, two quick tests)

1. **Low-voltage locked-rotor test with a torque arm on the output shaft** (supply current-limited, lock the output for a second or two at about 1-2 V, never at 12 V): the current gives R = V / I directly, and the force on the arm gives the output torque per ampere, Kt N eta [N.m/A]. The candidates predict 0.284 N.m/A (A, C), 0.355 N.m/A (method_d, eta 0.347) and 0.819 N.m/A (method_d2, eta 0.80), so this one measurement separates "lossy gearbox" from "practical rating".
2. **No-load speed versus voltage at 5-6 voltages** (with current): the slope gives Ke = K, and the intercept current gives Bm. This settles the Ke/Kt question (K = 1.97, 3.5, 5.7 or 6.4 mV.s/rad).

Open flags this resolves: is 7.5 A the stall current or a maximum rating, is 27.18 kg.cm theoretical or practical, and what is the real gearbox efficiency. None of the alternative sets is promoted to active until that data exists.
