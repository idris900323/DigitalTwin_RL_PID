\section{Discussion}
\paragraph*{What the evidence supports.} In simulation on the unvalidated twin, a PPO policy acting as a residual on a validation-tuned constant beat that constant by about $0.5$--$0.6$ median return (roughly $6$--$10\,\%$ of $|$return$|$), consistently across the three training seeds and on fresh scenarios. It is a modest gain on top of a good constant, not a demonstration that RL tunes PID gains well: the same policy captured only a minority of the oracle headroom, the benefit was weakest when payload and target change, and SAC and TD3 did not reproduce it.

\paragraph*{Why the unconstrained setup failed (hypotheses, not shown).} v2 differed from v3 in four ways at once: unconstrained multiplicative actions, an under-shaped reward, a checkpoint chosen on the training-evaluation stream, and early-peaking training. The experiments do not isolate which change mattered; the gains piled up at the bounds, suggesting that without a reference the policy exploits the cost structure at the edges of the action range.

\paragraph*{Adaptation or a better constant?} The learned gains varied little across scenarios (Section~\ref{sec:posthoc}). A residual that stays near a constant is still useful if that constant is better than a practical search finds, but then the contribution is a better tuning procedure, not scenario-aware adaptation. The post-hoc check addresses this; until its result is in, the headline should be read as ``a residual policy found a better operating point than the baseline search'' and not as evidence of adaptation.

\paragraph*{Practical guidance.} Report a validation-tuned constant and a hindsight oracle next to every learned tuner; they bracket what the learned policy contributes. Keep infeasible scenarios out of the headline; they dominate the variance of return and no controller can solve them. Treat success-rate gains with suspicion unless they replicate across seed sets.
