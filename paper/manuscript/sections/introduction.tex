\section{Introduction}
Tuning the position PID of a geared DC-motor joint is usually done by hand on hardware, starting from a manufacturer datasheet that is incomplete and sometimes internally inconsistent. Reinforcement learning (RL) has been proposed to tune or adapt PID gains automatically \cite{carlucho2017incremental,lawrence2021shallow}, but evaluations are often limited to simulation, to few random seeds and to weak baselines \cite{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021precipice}. A fair question is therefore not whether a learned tuner beats a poorly chosen preset, but whether it beats the best \emph{single} gain set that a modest search on the same scenarios would find, and by how much of what any scenario-aware tuner could achieve.

\paragraph*{What this paper does.}
On an open digital twin of a geared brushed DC-motor joint with a payload arm (Section~\ref{sec:problem}) we compare fixed and one-shot tuners with PPO, SAC and TD3 policies that adapt the gains every \SI{20}{ms} (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Every comparison is paired on the same scenarios, baselines and the reported policy seed are chosen on separate validation seeds, and the analysis rules (primary metric, one primary test, correction for the others, a separate table for infeasible scenarios) are stated before the confirmatory seeds are touched (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph*{What it finds.}
(i) An unconstrained multiplicative PPO setup failed against the validation-tuned constant and its training was unstable (Section~\ref{sec:v2}). (ii) A residual formulation, in which the action is an offset from that constant, improved on it modestly and consistently across training seeds and on fresh scenarios (Section~\ref{sec:v3}), but captured only part of the oracle headroom. (iii) SAC and TD3 did not reproduce the benefit (Section~\ref{sec:v4}). (iv) The learned policies end at gains that vary little across scenarios, which raises the question of how much of the benefit is adaptation rather than a better constant (Section~\ref{sec:posthoc}).

\paragraph*{Scope.}
Everything is simulated on a datasheet-derived twin that has not been compared with hardware; one motor model and one reward design are used, with three seeds per algorithm. The claims are therefore pre-validation, simulation-only claims. The hardware experiments that would test them are described as a replication protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:hardware} and contain no results.

\paragraph*{Contributions} (only those the evidence supports): a reproducible comparison protocol for learned PID adaptation with validation-selected baselines, exploratory and confirmatory seed sets and an oracle bound; the result that a residual PPO around a tuned constant beats that constant by about $0.5$--$0.6$ median return in simulation while capturing a minority of the oracle headroom; and negative results (an unconstrained setup, TD3, a mixed SAC) with the code, data and policies to check them.
