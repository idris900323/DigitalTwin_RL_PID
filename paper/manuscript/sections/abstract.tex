\begin{abstract}
Reports of learned PID tuners usually compare against weak baselines, on unvalidated models, with few seeds. We ask how much of an apparent reinforcement-learning (RL) gain survives a properly tuned constant baseline, on an open, datasheet-derived digital twin of a geared brushed DC-motor joint that we describe as \prelim: it has not been compared with hardware.
We propose a protocol: baselines and the reported policy seed are chosen on separate validation seeds, return is the primary metric, one primary test is declared in advance, infeasible scenarios are reported separately, and exploratory and confirmatory seed sets are labelled.
An unconstrained multiplicative PPO setup failed against a validation-tuned constant. A residual PPO around that constant improved on it by $\ConfThreeStaticMed$, $\ConfThreeChangesMed$ and $\ConfThreeOodMed$ median return (static, payload and target changes, out-of-distribution) on fresh confirmatory seeds, for all three training seeds. However, \emph{a much larger validation search for the constant alone} recovers most of that gain on further fresh seeds ($\SensVThreeStaticBestConstantExtBestConstantMed$, $\SensVThreeChangesBestConstantExtBestConstantMed$, $\SensVThreeOodBestConstantExtBestConstantMed$ median return over the original constant), and a simple non-learned rule conditioned on payload mass, gravity torque and inertia matches the policy (policy minus rule: $\SensVThreeStaticPpoScenarioFitMed$, $\SensVThreeChangesPpoScenarioFitMed$, $\SensVThreeOodPpoScenarioFitMed$, intervals including zero). The policy's remaining advantage over the stronger constant is at most $\SensVThreeStaticPpoBestConstantExtMed$ median return, and zero in the payload-change regime. TD3 did not beat the constant and SAC was mixed. All results are simulated on an unvalidated twin; the hardware part is a replication protocol without results. Code, policies, per-episode data and scripts that regenerate every table and figure are provided.
\end{abstract}
